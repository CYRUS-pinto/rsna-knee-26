- [4] **Clarification on Training Data** This is my current understanding. There's no proper ground truth "label" here. A radiologist reads an MRI and writes a report, but scans are hard to read and two radiologists can disagree. That happens in every medical imaging competition, you're just not given the report, so you take the label as truth when it isn't. What's different is the host told us how the labels were made: two readers against a published rubric, a third adjudicating, same for the test set. So there is a procedure and it disagrees with the reports in a known direction: the readers use stricter thresholds than a radiologist writing for clinical care, so the reports over-call. The reports are arguably a richer dataset than usual, since a report says more than a binary label. The reason you'd want more than the ~50 provided labels is to calibrate your extractor against the competition's annotators - you're trying to match your label generator to theirs, which is hard with so few.
